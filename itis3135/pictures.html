Everything Pictures

This page is for how images work on the web. Each of the two photos below goes through the same set of experiments which include converting formats, shrinking with CSS, shrinking properly in an editor, distorting proportions on purpose, and enlarging a small copy. Seeing the file sizes, dimensions and quality side by side shows why a 5000 pixel photo should never be sent to a browser untouched, and why proportions matter.

Hong Kong's Neon Skyline: Demonstrating Image Manipulation With a Free Photo From The Web

I picked this photo because the lit up windows, thin antenna lines and reflections on the water are fine details that show every flaw when an image is shrunk, stretched or enlarged badly. It was taken by Lance He, a photographer with many similar vibrant photos, and downloaded from Pexels. It shows the Hong Kong skyline at night over Victoria Harbour, in purple tones. The original is a JPG at 5568 × 3712 pixels and 3.71 MB.

Circle Next to Head: Demonstrating Proportions with My Own Photo

I picked a round black plate because a circle has exactly one correct proportion, while a human head does not. If the plate looks like an oval, I know right away that the image has been distorted. I took this photo myself in my kitchen, holding the plate next to my face. It is a portrait (tall) JPG at 2316 × 3088 pixels and 944 KB, so it shows what happens to a phone photo that gets squeezed into the wrong shape.
